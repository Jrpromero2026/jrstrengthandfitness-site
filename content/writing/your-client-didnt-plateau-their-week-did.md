Week six. Your client's squat hasn't moved, the scale hasn't moved, and their energy in session is flat. The reflex is to rewrite the program: a new split, a new rep scheme, something to shake it loose.

Most of the time, the program isn't what changed.

Before I touch a single set or rep, I look at the week around the training. It's the fastest way I know to avoid fixing something that isn't broken.

## Is it the program, or the week?

A program that worked for five weeks rarely stops working on its own. Something upstream moved: sleep got shorter, work got louder, sessions got skipped or squeezed. Change the program in the middle of that and you're adjusting the wrong variable. Worse, you can't tell what fixed it.

So the order matters. Rule out the week first. Change the plan last.

> If you change the program every time the week gets hard, you never learn whether the program worked.
> — JR Prieto-Romero

## What do I check before changing the plan?

Four questions, in this order. If one of them is the answer, I fix that and leave the program alone.

1. **Attendance.** Did they actually train the sessions as written? A missed day here and there adds up fast in a block built on frequency.
2. **Sleep.** Not a perfect number, just the honest trend. Ask what changed, not whether it's "good."
3. **Life load.** New job, new baby, a deadline, a move. Stress doesn't care that it didn't come from the barbell.
4. **Effort.** Are the working sets still hard? Sometimes the weight stayed the same because nobody asked for more.

Only when all four come back clean do I start looking at the program itself.

## What changes when you coach this way?

Clients stop seeing their training as a pile of programs that didn't work. They see one plan that held up, and a coach who found the real problem. That's what keeps people around long enough to get results.

## Your move: run the check-in this week

:::copy The check-in
1. How many of this week's sessions did you get in as written?
2. How has your sleep compared to a normal week for you?
3. Anything new outside the gym taking up your energy right now?
4. On your last working sets, how many more reps did you have left?
:::

Ask before you write the next block, and write the answers down. If one stands out, deal with that first.
