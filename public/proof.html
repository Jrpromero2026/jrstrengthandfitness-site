Read Yiğit’s full story
Starting point

Before I started training with JR, I had little to no experience with the gym or living a healthy lifestyle. I had worked with a few personal trainers back in my home country, but they never really pushed me into serious strength training.

I had never deadlifted before, couldn’t do a single pull-up without resistance bands, and I still remember my legs shaking during lower-body exercises as I struggled just to keep my balance.

I wasn’t necessarily starting from zero, but I was pretty close to it.

The plan

Our plan was simple: get stronger.

I never showed JR pictures of someone whose physique I wanted to achieve. I never asked him to make me look like a particular person. I just told him one thing: I wanted to be strong.

And that happened to be one of his greatest strengths as a trainer: knowing how to build real strength through the right training, proper technique, and consistent progression.

We started training four times a week, eventually increasing to five sessions a week about a year later. The goal was always the same: keep getting stronger, keep pushing my limits, and keep improving.

The physical transformation, healthier eating habits, and everything else? Those came along as welcome bonuses.

Where now

These days, one of the first things I think about when I wake up is training. It has become such a natural part of my life that I can’t really imagine my routine without it.

Of course, it comes with its own funny little downsides. Every time I eat, I find myself thinking about calories. But then again, I train more so I can eat more. And the more I train, the stronger I get. So I guess it all works out!

But beyond the physical progress, training has completely changed my outlook on life.

I’ve come to realize that working out isn’t something you do for a few months or years and then quit once you’re happy with the results. It’s a lifestyle. It’s about constantly challenging yourself, discovering what you’re capable of, and realizing that there’s always room to improve.

And if you’re wondering when you finally reach that point where you’re completely satisfied?

That’s the neat part. You don’t.
