The first mistake after a long break is starting where you left off. The second is starting from zero, like you've never trained.

You keep more than you think, and it comes back faster than it took to build. The first month back is about getting there without getting hurt or discouraged.

## What you keep

Strength and muscle drop when you stop training, but they don't disappear, and they return quickly. I covered the detraining research in [how quickly you lose strength gains](/notes/how-quickly-you-lose-strength-gains).

The retraining research is just as encouraging. In one study, women who had strength trained for 20 weeks took 30 to 32 weeks off, then retrained for six weeks. They regained strength and muscle fiber size quickly, and the researchers concluded that some adaptations are kept for long periods and help people get back to form fast.[1] In another, men who took three weeks off twice during six months of training ended up with similar muscle growth to men who trained straight through.[2]

So the work you did before still counts. Your job now is to bring it back without rushing.

## Weeks 1 and 2: get your rhythm back

**Train two or three days a week.** Full-body sessions built around a few main lifts.

**Start well below your old weights.** A good starting point is roughly half to two-thirds of what you used to lift for the same reps. It should feel too easy. That's fine.

**Do fewer sets.** Two sets per exercise is plenty for now.

**Stop well short of failure.** Leave several reps in the tank on every set.

**Expect some soreness.** The first week back almost always brings it. Soreness that fades in a day or two is normal.

The goal of these two weeks is showing up, moving well and leaving every session feeling like you could have done more.

## Weeks 3 and 4: start building

**Add load when your reps look clean.** If the last set felt easy and your form held, go up a little next time.

**Add a set to your main lifts.** Go from two sets to three on the exercises that matter most to you.

**Bring back harder sets gradually.** By the end of week four, your last set of a main lift can get closer to hard.

After four weeks you'll have a much better idea of where you really are, and you can return to a normal program or set a new goal.

## Signs you're moving too fast

- Soreness that's still there at your next session
- Joint pain, or pain that changes how you move
- Dreading sessions, or skipping them because the last one wrecked you

If any of those show up, take the weights back down for a week. There's no prize for getting back to your old numbers in week two.

## Make the next break shorter

Most long breaks start as a busy month. If you have a plan ready for those stretches, they're less likely to turn into six months off. I wrote about [the minimum training plan you can actually maintain](/notes/the-minimum-training-plan-you-can-maintain) for exactly that.
