After a rough month, most people step on the scale or test one lift, see a bad number and decide they've lost everything. Usually they haven't. They've had a bad month, and they're reading it off a single bad day.

## Separate noise from real change

**Body weight moves for a lot of reasons.** Salt, carbs, water, travel, sleep and stress can push the scale up or down a few pounds in a day or two. One weigh-in after a rough week says very little.

**Strength on any given day depends on recovery.** Stress affects how you recover from training. In one study of college students in resistance training classes, those under more stress recovered muscle function more slowly over the four days after a hard leg session.[1] If you test a lift during a stressful week, you're measuring the week as much as your strength.

**Real loss takes longer than most people think.** I covered the research in [how quickly you lose strength gains](/notes/how-quickly-you-lose-strength-gains). Trained people can hold strength through a few weeks of no training at all. A month of reduced or patchy training costs much less than a month off.

## Look at the month, not the day

Instead of one number, look at what actually happened:

- How many sessions did you get in compared with what you planned?
- Did you keep your main lifts in the plan, even at lower weights?
- How were sleep and stress, honestly?
- Did anything hurt, or was it just hard?

If you trained half as often as planned, kept your main lifts and nothing hurt, you held most of what you had. That's a good month given the circumstances.

When you do check numbers, use averages and trends. Weigh in on several mornings and look at the average. Compare your working sets over two or three weeks, not one heavy single on your worst day.

## Plan the next month

Once you've read the month fairly, decide what the next one needs.

**Life is calming down.** Go back to your normal plan. If you missed a lot, take the weights down slightly for the first week and build back.

**Life is still busy.** Switch to a minimum plan on purpose, instead of trying to run your full plan and missing half of it. I wrote about [building one you can actually maintain](/notes/the-minimum-training-plan-you-can-maintain).

**Something hurt.** Get it looked at before you push again.

A difficult month is part of training for years. The goal is to come out of it still training, with an honest picture of where you are. For one hard week inside a normal month, see [how to adjust training during a high-stress week](/notes/how-to-adjust-training-during-a-high-stress-week).
