Maintenance gets treated like giving up. It's a training phase like any other, and sometimes it's the smartest one you can choose.

The problem is that most people end up in maintenance by accident. Training slides, progress stops, and they feel like they're failing. Choosing it on purpose, for a set amount of time, is a completely different experience.

## When maintenance is the right call

**When life is full.** A new job, a move, a new baby, a heavy season at work. Trying to set records through all of that usually means burning out on training or on everything else.

**After a long push.** If you've been building for months, a few weeks of holding steady lets your body and your head catch up before the next block.

**During a sport season.** Athletes maintain in season so they can perform. Adults with a race, a ski season or a summer league can do the same thing.

**When you're where you want to be.** Not everyone needs to keep chasing more. If you're strong, healthy and happy with how you look and move, holding that is a real goal.

I use an intervention hierarchy in my coaching: Inadequate, Adequate, Optimal and Enhanced. Not everyone needs to be pushing toward Enhanced all the time. Adequate is a legitimate target, not a failure, and so is a stretch spent holding what you've built.

## What maintenance takes

Less than building. A 2021 review found that strength and muscle size can be maintained for up to 32 weeks with as little as one strength session a week and one set per exercise, in younger people, as long as intensity stays high. The authors note that older adults may need more, closer to two sessions a week with a few more sets.[1]

The part people get wrong is intensity. Maintenance means fewer sessions and fewer sets. It doesn't mean light weights. Keep lifting heavy enough that the sets are genuinely hard.

## What to keep doing

**Your main lifts.** Keep the movements that matter most to you in the plan, so you're not relearning them later.

**The effort.** Fewer, harder sets beat lots of easy ones here.

**The basics outside the gym.** Sleep and protein still matter. My nutrition position is simple: protein has a floor, fat has a base, carbs fill the rest. Keep the protein floor even when the rest of your week is a mess.

## Make it a decision

**Set an end date or a review date.** Four weeks, eight weeks, the end of the season. Put it on the calendar.

**Define what holding looks like.** Your main lifts within a few reps of where they are now. Body weight in a range you're comfortable with. Two sessions a week, every week.

**Know what comes next.** When the review date comes, decide whether you're going back to building, staying in maintenance, or changing goals.
