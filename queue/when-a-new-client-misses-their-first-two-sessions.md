Two missed sessions in a new client's first few weeks is a warning. Most trainers treat it as a scheduling problem and wait for the client to rebook. By the time they notice, the client is gone.

The first five sessions decide whether a client stays. A client who misses two of them is telling you something about the plan, the schedule or the first experience. Your job is to find out which before the third miss.

## What a missed session usually means

Missed sessions are information. The common reasons are simple:

- The schedule never fit. The client booked times they hoped would work.
- The first session was too much. They were sore for four days and dreaded the next one.
- They don't know what they're working toward. Nothing connects this session to their goal.
- Life happened, and nobody made it easy to come back.

Only one of those is out of your hands, and even that one you can make easier.

## What to do

**Reach out the same day.** A short message, no guilt. "Missed you today. Everything OK? Want me to hold Thursday at 6 for you?" The point is that someone noticed.

**Ask one question.** "What got in the way?" Then listen. The answer tells you which problem you're solving.

**Fix the schedule before the program.** If the times don't fit, book the next two sessions at times they're sure they can keep, even if it means fewer sessions a week for now. Two sessions they make beat three they skip.

**Make the next session easy to say yes to.** Shorter, lighter, and built around something they can do well. A client who leaves the third session feeling capable comes back for the fourth.

**Check how the first sessions felt.** If they were wrecked afterward, that's on the programming. Start lighter than you think and build loads up across the first weeks. I wrote about that in [how to turn an assessment into programming decisions](/notes/how-to-turn-an-assessment-into-programming-decisions).

**Reconnect the plan to their goal.** Tell them what the first block is for, in one sentence, and what they'll notice if it works.

## What not to do

Don't guilt them. Don't wait for them to come to you. And don't try to make up for missed sessions with a harder one. Intensity is the wrong tool in the first month.

## Build it into your system

Motivation is temporary, and that includes yours. Don't leave this to whether you remember. Make it a standard:

- Any new client who misses a session gets a message that day.
- Any new client who misses two of their first five gets a call or a conversation before their next booking.
- Write down what got in the way and what you changed.

That record pays off. After a few months you'll see patterns, like a booking time that never works or a first session that's too much for most people, and you can fix them before the next client walks in.
